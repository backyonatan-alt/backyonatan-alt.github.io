# Wooden pirate ship playgrounds in Israel (1980s-90s): research notes

Status: 19 Nov 2026. Working database is `ships.csv` (29 entries).

## Confidence levels in the CSV
- `confirmed-municipality` / `confirmed-press` / `confirmed-local-press` / `confirmed-web`: a named published source.
- `memories-many`: 4 or more independent Facebook commenters.
- `memories-few` / `memory-single`: 1 to 3 commenters. Leads only.

Nothing here is verified for build year, dimensions or builder. Do not publish those fields as facts.

## Weak single mentions, not in the CSV
Shlomi, Beit Shemesh ("Gan HaEtz"), Ness Ziona, Ramat Gan, Lod, Bat Yam, Kiryat Binyamin, Eilat (Ofir), Park HaYarkon (user memory; no source found, probably Sarig's log Adventure Garden).

## Patterns
- Everyone believed their town's ship was unique.
- Fire ended many of them: Kiryat Shmona, Ramla, Ariel, Dimona, Afula Illit, Eilat, Kokhav Yair (2007), Kiryat Malakhi.
- Enclosed lower deck turned into a toilet / drug spot at night in several towns.
- The mast was the dare.
- Mostly development towns in the north and south.
- 1988 recurs: Eilat ("installed 1988"), Migdal HaEmek ("the playground of 88"), Hai-Kef opened 1988. 1988 was Israel's 40th year. Hypothesis only.
- Shared design in photos (Migdal HaEmek, Dimona, Kiryat Shmona): two masts with slatted crow's nests, raised stern with panels, side ramp. Dimona's hull carried a "Captain Hook" sign. Points to one design or one supplier. Unconfirmed.
- Rings climbing structure next to the ship in Afula; "Park HaTabaot" (Rings Park) in Migdal HaEmek. Possibly sold as a set.

## Context: why log playgrounds appeared and vanished
- Landscape architect Gideon Sarig built Israel's first Adventure Garden from reused telephone poles in Park HaYarkon, 1971 (Rokach Prize 1973), after developing the idea in Berkeley in 1966.
- Sarig (mako, 2014): log elements lasted until the 1990s, then disappeared; the Standards Institute approves only maximum safety.
- No source links Sarig to the ships themselves.
