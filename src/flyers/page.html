Frequent Flyers

An NBA team flies about 45,000 miles in a season, twice around the Earth. Each dot below is a team. Press play and watch all 30 of them criss-cross the country for six months. Then see what the miles actually cost.

The story in brief

Does all that flying cost them games?

The NBA plays 82 games in 174 days across four time zones, and nobody plays at home more than half the time. Add it up and a team flies around 45,000 miles a season. The Spurs flew 57,000. The Pistons, 35,000.

Players, coaches and fans all believe the travel matters. West coast teams complain about early tip-offs in the East. East coast teams dread the long western swing. The league redesigned its schedule in recent years to cut back-to-backs and bundle road trips.

This page rebuilds every team's route from the 2024-25 schedule, 1,230 games arena to arena, and then checks the belief against the results. The short version: the miles don't cost wins. The nights without rest do.

3 · Does it hurt?

Road teams win about 45% of games and home teams 55%. That gap is well known. The question is what inside the road game explains it: the flight, the time zones, or the lack of sleep. Here are all 4,920 road games of the last four seasons, split three ways.

Road games only, 2021-22 to 2024-25. Rest is full days between games. Distance and time zones are measured from the previous game venue. Green and red mark groups that differ from the road average by more than two standard errors. Grey ones are within chance.

A road team on no rest wins 40%. With a day off, 46%. The distance makes no difference at all.

The pattern holds in every one of the four seasons: back-to-back road teams won between 35% and 42% each year, rested road teams between 42% and 51%. Distance and time zones never separate from the average.

Good teams might simply get easier schedules, so I also fitted a model that accounts for how strong each team and its opponent were that season, plus home court. The result is the same.

Effects are shown in win-percentage points for an even matchup. "Confidence" summarizes the standard error: very high means the effect is at least five times its error, low means under two.

This fits what sleep researchers have found: the body cares about hours of rest and the clock it is on, far more than about miles. A 2,500 mile flight followed by a day off is easier than a 300 mile hop straight into a game. It also explains why the league's schedule changes in recent years targeted back-to-backs rather than total miles.

4 · How this was counted

  • Schedule and results: ESPN data for the 2024-25 regular season, via the sportsdataverse hoopR project. 1,230 games, including the NBA Cup final in Las Vegas, two Pacers games in Paris and the Spurs' home games in Austin.
  • Distance: great-circle miles between the arenas of consecutive games, counted from the team's home arena at the start and back to it at the end. No flights home mid-trip, so real totals are somewhat higher.
  • Time zones: standard-time offsets of each arena. Daylight saving changes are ignored.
  • Rest: full calendar days between a team's consecutive games. Zero means a back-to-back.
  • Chapters 1 and 2 use the 2024-25 season. Chapter 3 pools four seasons, 2021-22 to 2024-25, 9,840 team-games, and fits a logistic regression of the result on home court, team strength, opponent strength, rest, distance and time zones crossed.
  • Not done: minutes played by starters, injuries, and the hour of tip-off, all of which also travel with the schedule.
