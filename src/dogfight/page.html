Dogfight Ground School

You don't get on a tail by pointing at the other jet. You get there by flying to where it was, at the speed where your jet turns best.

The story in brief

Why does the jet you are chasing keep getting away?

Anyone who has tried a flight simulator knows the moment. You spot the enemy, point your nose straight at it, and it slides off the screen. You turn harder. It gets further away. Pointing at the other jet is the natural thing to do, and it is the first thing fighter pilots are taught to unlearn.

A dogfight is a close turning fight between two aircraft. Air forces teach it under the name basic fighter maneuvers, or BFM. Under the jargon it is geometry. Every turning jet flies a circle, and the winner is the one who gets onto the other jet's circle, behind it, at the speed where their own jet turns best.

This page is the ground school nobody gave you. It covers the handful of ideas that turn a beginner into someone who can get on a tail and stay there: how to read the HUD, pursuit geometry, speed, six classic maneuvers, weapons, and drills you can fly in any simulator. Every maneuver has a 3D clip you can pause and scrub, and you can fly the chase yourself.

Read the HUD first

Every sim puts the same few numbers on the glass in front of you, and most beginners never look at them. This is a HUD frozen in the middle of a lost fight: 2.1 km behind an enemy jet that is getting away. The numbers already show why. Read them as if you were the pilot:

584Airspeed in knots. Far too fast to turn. Target: about 450.
G 2.0You're barely pulling. At this speed and G your turn circle is about 5 km wide in radius.
M 0.92Mach number. Same story as the airspeed.
THR 60%Throttle. Fine, but the speed hasn't bled off yet.
R 2.1 KMRange to the selected target. Close enough for a missile, if the angle were right.
325Heading. The right tape (11,835) is altitude in feet.
MSL 4Missiles left. Ammo is not the problem here. The setup is.

Three fixes, in order of payoff: slow to about 450 knots before you turn, pull hard when you do turn (7 to 9 G, not 2), and aim behind the bandit, not at it. The next two sections show why.

Pursuit geometry

A turning jet flies a circle. To sit on its tail you have to get onto that same circle, behind it. Where you put your nose decides whether that happens:

  • Lead pursuit: nose ahead of the bandit. Closes distance fast and cuts the corner. Needed for a gun shot. Held too long, you fly out in front.
  • Pure pursuit: nose on the bandit. Feels natural, used for a missile shot. With extra speed you end up flying through them.
  • Lag pursuit: nose behind the bandit's tail. Closes slowly, keeps you behind, and lets you match their circle. This is the one most new pilots miss.

Speed is your turn

Too fast and the circle is huge. Too slow and the wings can't pull enough G. In between sits corner speed, the slowest speed where you can still pull maximum G. That is where the jet turns fastest and tightest at once. For an F-16-type jet it lands around 400 to 450 knots.

The bar is 2.1 km, the range in the screenshot. At 584 kt and 9 G the radius is about 1,030 m. At the 2 G in the screenshot it is about 5,300 m.

Pulling G costs speed. A hard turn bleeds knots every second. So the throttle rule in a turning fight is simple: above 480, throttle back or tap the airbrake. Between 400 and 480, leave it. Below 400, go full throttle and burner and keep pulling. Below 300, ease off the pull for two seconds with the nose slightly down and burner on, then turn again.

Altitude is stored speed. Nose down gives you knots for free, nose up spends them. That is what the yo-yos below use.

The numbers come from a standard physics model, not from any particular sim. Drill 2 measures where your jet's corner speed actually sits in yours.

Controls, whatever you fly on

Keyboard, gamepad or a full stick: the inputs are the same eight things. Technique is about the order you use them in. Find each one in your sim's control list before you start.

Roll, then pullEvery turn is two steps. Roll until the bandit is at the top of your screen, stop rolling, then pull. The jet turns toward the top of its canopy (pilots call it the lift vector). Don't try to steer with the rudder.
Padlock or target viewSelect the bandit and keep your eyes on it. Lose sight, lose the fight. If the padlock view confuses you, cycle views until one keeps both jets on screen.
ThrottleSet it before the turn, not during. Glance at the airspeed box on the left of the HUD every few seconds and hold 400 to 480 knots.
AfterburnerUse it to climb, to recover below 400 knots, to run down a bandit that is flying straight, or to escape. Never while already above 500 in a turn.
AirbrakeShort taps to stop an overshoot. It throws energy away, so a high yo-yo is the better fix when you have the room.
Rudder (yaw)Only for the last few degrees of a gun shot. Never to turn the jet.
Missile and gunSee Weapons. Both need a steady nose, so stop rolling first.
Flares and radarFlares with a break turn when someone is behind you. Radar to find the next contact.

Which way is "pull" depends on your sim. On most, pulling the stick back (or the down arrow on a keyboard) raises the nose. "Pull" on this page always means nose toward the top of your canopy.

Maneuvers

Six moves cover almost every situation. Each one is the same roll-then-pull, aimed somewhere different. Every clip loops like a short video. Pause it, or drag the bar to any moment. The steps light up as the jet flies them.

High yo-yo

You're behind a turning bandit but too fast. It's sliding across your windscreen and you're about to fly past. Going up slows you down and shrinks your circle without throwing energy away.

    Low yo-yo

    You're behind but can't close. The bandit sits at the same range, turning as well as you. The dive adds speed and cuts across the inside of the bandit's circle.

      Break turn

      A bandit or a missile is behind you. This is the first defensive move, always. Turning into the attacker makes the angle too sharp for them to follow, and they cross in front of your 3-9 line.

        Scissors

        After an overshoot you're side by side, both slow, both turning into each other. Each reversal is a race to go forward more slowly than the other jet.

          Immelmann and Split-S

          You need to reverse direction fast, right after a head-on pass or to escape. Both are half loops with a half roll to get upright again. Immelmann when you're fast (450+), Split-S when you're slow and high. Never Split-S below about 8,000 ft or above 450 kt. The loop down is big.

            The merge

            You and the bandit pass head-on. Whoever turns better in the next 15 seconds gets the tail. Same G, different speed: the slower jet at corner speed gets its nose around first.

              Weapons

              Missile. A heat-seeker wants the bandit's tailpipe and a steady launch. Best shot: you're behind its 3-9 line, 1 to 3 km back, nose on it (pure pursuit), wings steady for a second. The screenshot range of 2.1 km is fine. What was missing is the angle.

              Gun. Inside about 600 m. Bullets take time to fly, so put your nose ahead of the bandit along its path (lead pursuit) and let it fly into the stream. Short bursts. Lead pursuit closes fast, so plan to pull up and away right after.

              The sequence that works: lag to get behind and stable, pure for the missile, and only if that fails, lead for the gun.
