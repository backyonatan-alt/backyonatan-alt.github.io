Dogfight Ground School

You don't get on a tail by pointing at the other jet. You get there by flying to where it was, at the speed where your jet turns best. Everything below is that one idea, broken down.

The story in brief

Why does the jet you are chasing keep getting away?

Point your nose straight at an enemy jet and you will probably lose it. That feels wrong, and it is one of the first things fighter pilots are taught to unlearn.

A dogfight is a close turning fight between two aircraft. Air forces teach it under the name basic fighter maneuvers, or BFM. Under the jargon it is geometry. Every turning jet flies a circle, and the winner is the one who gets onto the other jet's circle, behind it, at the speed where their own jet turns best.

This page started as a training guide for Epic Fury IAF, a flight game played on a keyboard. It opens with one real screenshot from a lost fight, then explains pursuit geometry, speed, six classic maneuvers, weapons and a set of drills. You can fly the chase yourself in the animation and drag the speed chart.

Three fixes, in order of payoff: slow to about 450 knots before you turn, pull hard when you do turn (7 to 9 G, not 2), and aim behind the bandit, not at it. The next two sections show why.

Pursuit geometry

A turning jet flies a circle. To sit on its tail you have to get onto that same circle, behind it. Where you put your nose decides whether that happens:

  • Lead pursuit: nose ahead of the bandit. Closes distance fast and cuts the corner. Needed for a gun shot. Held too long, you fly out in front.
  • Pure pursuit: nose on the bandit. Feels natural, used for a missile shot. With extra speed you end up flying through them.
  • Lag pursuit: nose behind the bandit's tail. Closes slowly, keeps you behind, and lets you match their circle. This is the one most new pilots miss.

Speed is your turn

Too fast and the circle is huge. Too slow and the wings can't pull enough G. In between sits corner speed, the slowest speed where you can still pull maximum G. That is where the jet turns fastest and tightest at once. For an F-16-type jet it lands around 400 to 450 knots.

The bar is 2.1 km, the range in the screenshot. At 584 kt and 9 G the radius is about 1,030 m. At the 2 G in the screenshot it is about 5,300 m.

Pulling G costs speed. A hard turn bleeds knots every second. So the throttle rule in a turning fight is simple: above 480, throttle back or tap the airbrake. Between 400 and 480, leave it. Below 400, go full throttle and burner and keep pulling. Below 300, ease off the pull for two seconds with the nose slightly down and burner on, then turn again.

Altitude is stored speed. Nose down gives you knots for free, nose up spends them. That is what the yo-yos below use.

Keyboard technique

On a keyboard every input is all or nothing, so technique is about order and timing.

← → roll, then ↑/↓ pullEvery turn is two steps. Roll until the bandit is at the top of your screen, release roll, then hold the pull key. The jet turns toward the top of its canopy (the lift vector). Don't try to steer with yaw.
P padlock, T [ ] targetSelect the bandit and keep eyes on it. Lose sight, lose the fight. If the padlock view confuses you, tap V views until one keeps both jets visible.
Z X / scroll: throttleSet it before the turn. Glance at the speed box on the left of the HUD every few seconds and hold 400 to 480.
Shift boost (afterburner)Use it to climb, to recover below 400 kt, to run down a bandit that's flying straight, or to escape. Never while already above 500 in a turn.
B airbrakeShort taps to stop an overshoot. It throws away energy, so a high yo-yo is the better fix when you have the room.
Q E yawOnly for the last few degrees of a gun shot. Never to turn the jet.
Space missile, Ctrl/Tab gunSee Weapons. Both need a steady nose, so release roll first.
F flare, R radarFlares with a break turn when someone is behind you. Radar to find the next contact.

Check which arrow raises the nose in your game. In most sims it is ↓ (stick back). "Pull" below always means nose toward the top of your canopy.

Maneuvers

Six moves cover almost every situation. Each one is the same roll-then-pull, aimed somewhere different.

High yo-yo

You're behind a turning bandit but too fast. It's sliding across your windscreen and you're about to fly past.

  1. Ease the roll so your wings are about halfway to level.
  2. Pull. The nose goes up and outside the turn, and speed drops.
  3. As the bandit passes below your nose, roll back toward it and pull down onto its tail.

Low yo-yo

You're behind but can't close. The bandit sits at the same range, turning as well as you.

  1. Roll a little past the bandit so your nose points below and ahead of it (lead).
  2. Pull. You dive across the inside of the turn and gain speed.
  3. Roll back, pull up to the bandit. Don't hold the dive long or you'll overshoot.

Break turn

A bandit or a missile is behind you. This is the first defensive move, always.

  1. Roll toward the side the attacker is on and pull as hard as you can. Tap F a few times.
  2. Keep pulling. You're trading speed for angle on purpose.
  3. When they flash past, reverse your roll and turn after them. Now you're the one behind.

Scissors

After an overshoot you're side by side, both slow, both turning into each other.

  1. Throttle back, airbrake out, and turn toward the bandit.
  2. Each time you cross its path, reverse your roll and pull back toward it.
  3. The moment it drifts ahead of your wing line, stop weaving and point at its tail.

Immelmann and Split-S

You need to reverse direction fast, right after a head-on pass or to escape.

  1. Immelmann (you're fast, 450+): burner, pull straight up and over, then roll upright at the top.
  2. Split-S (you're slow and high): roll upside down, throttle back, pull through to level.
  3. Never Split-S below about 8,000 ft or above 450 kt. The loop down is big.

The merge

You and the bandit pass head-on. Whoever turns better in the next 15 seconds gets the tail.

  1. Arrive at 450 kt. Pass close, don't turn early.
  2. As the bandit passes your wing, roll toward it and pull maximum G.
  3. Halfway around, look for it. If your nose is coming onto it first, you won the turn. Go to lag and settle in.

Weapons

Missile (Space). A heat-seeker wants the bandit's tailpipe and a steady launch. Best shot: you're behind its 3-9 line, 1 to 3 km back, nose on it (pure pursuit), wings steady for a second. The screenshot range of 2.1 km is fine. What was missing is the angle. The game's own loading tips say the same: confirm the selected contact before firing, and keep moving during the attack.

Gun (Ctrl or Tab). Inside about 600 m. Bullets take time to fly, so put your nose ahead of the bandit along its path (lead pursuit) and let it fly into the stream. Short bursts. Lead pursuit closes fast, so plan to pull up and away right after.

The sequence that works: lag to get behind and stable, pure for the missile, and only if that fails, lead for the gun.
