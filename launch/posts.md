## LinkedIn / X

I asked an AI a simple question: how related are two random Jews?

The answer sent me down a rabbit hole. Geneticists say today's 10 million Ashkenazi Jews carry the DNA signature of a founding group equivalent to about 350 people, who lived 25 to 32 generations ago. Any two of them share about as much DNA as fourth or fifth cousins.

So I built a map to see how that happened. 3,000 years, 36 regions, 9 communities. Press play and watch Babylon, Alexandria, Spain, Poland and New York rise and fall.

Three things that surprised me:
1. In 1100, Ashkenazi Jews were a few percent of the Jewish world. By 1939 they were nine in ten.
2. The world Jewish population today (15.8M) is still smaller than in 1939 (16.6M).
3. Communities that lasted 2,500 years in Iraq and Yemen left the map in about three years.

The numbers are estimates and the older ones are rough. If you know this field, I'd love corrections.

## Reddit

Title (r/dataisbeautiful): [OC] 3,000 years of the Jewish population by region and genetic community, animated

First comment:

Interactive version (play, zoom, change speed): https://backyonatan-alt.github.io/cousins/

Sources: world totals from DellaPergola / American Jewish Year Book (1880 onward) and Botticini & Eckstein, The Chosen Few (65 to 1490). Genetics from Carmi et al. 2014, Waldman et al. 2022, Atzmon et al. 2010, Behar et al. 2006. Full list on the page.

Tool: plain HTML canvas and JavaScript, built with Claude. Map outlines from Natural Earth.

Caveats: circle area is population. Regional figures before 1800 are my reconstruction from standard estimates, so read them as orders of magnitude. First-century figures use the high-end estimate of about 5 million, which many archaeologists think is too high. The timeline is stretched so 1939 to 1945 gets as much room as several centuries of late antiquity.

Corrections welcome, I'll update the data.
